TFL Installation

LONDONS COMMUNITY runs like clockwork. With roughly 5 Million passenger journeys made in London on a busy day. We spend hours commuting to-and-from work, to different boroughs and the other side of the Thames. In 1863 the first tube line was opened and over time TFL expanded in central London and into it’s outskirts. The underground has shaped London’s physical and economical geography. Creating suburbs and connecting communities.

Londoners dependence on the tube is quickly realized when we face any disturbance. Ageing infrastructure, out of date system and TFL employees striking for better work conditions, cancellations and delays have become the daily norm and strikes halting our day.

The following installation is driven by live TFL data. Each tube line is visible with lights flickering and shifting colours in real time. Station positions are displayed along the tube route. The changing colours and energy of the installations displays the tubes severity status. This installation makes visible what Londoners experience daily. A NETWORK UNDER STRAIN. By mapping live disruption data onto light and colour, the installation invites the viewer to sit with the reality of a city dependent on infrastructure that increasingly fails it.

At what point does unreliability stop being an inconvenience and start being a crisis?
